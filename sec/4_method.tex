\subsection{Formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}

Let $x$ be a footprint with surface state $s(x)$. The corpus observes $s$ in two ways. Static maps $X_m$ measure one aspect of the state directly. Camera images $X_{m,g}$ depend on the state and on the acquisition geometry $g$, six angles that fix the sun and the viewing direction. \method has an encoder $f_\theta$ that estimates a token-grid representation of the state from one partially masked observation $X_s$ of the footprint, and a predictor $g_\psi$ that, given the identity of a target modality $t$ and its geometry $g_t$, predicts how $t$ looks in representation space. The representation it must match is produced by an EMA copy of the encoder from the actual target observation, which the student never sees. Geometry plays the part that actions play in a world model~\cite{lecun2022path}: the same state rolled out under a different sun must give a different image. The encoder is not told the sun of its source; only the predictor is told the sun of its target, and static targets are predicted with a null geometry. Each training row pairs one source with $M{=}2$ targets (\cref{sec:tasks}).

\subsection{One token, one ground cell}
\label{sec:tokens}

A common approach resamples all modalities to a shared canvas and cuts it into patches. At a $64\times64$ token grid with $4\times4$ patches, this shrinks the 1000\,px topography raster four times and the 600\,px WAC image 2.3 times, and blows the 60\,px roughness map up four times. We instead align tokens to ground cells, as OmniSat and AnySat do~\cite{astruc2024omnisat,astruc2025anysat}, and give every modality $m$ its own stem $\phi_m$ whose kernel and stride equal its native pixels per token,
\begin{equation}
  p_m = \max\!\big(k_{\min},\ \operatorname{round}(\min(E/r_m, S_{\max})/G)\big),
  \label{eq:ppt}
\end{equation}
where $E{=}60$\,km is the footprint extent, $r_m$ the ground sample distance, $S_{\max}$ a cap on the raster side and $G$ the grid size. Sub-tile rows (NAC, stereo terrain) use $E{=}500$\,m in the same rule, which gives the 500\,px NAC and 166\,px terrain rasters. The raster is resized to $Gp_m$ pixels per side, which changes it by a few percent unless the floor applies, and $\phi_m$ maps it to a $G\times G$ grid of $D$-dimensional tokens. Every token is then one ground cell of $E/G$ meters (940\,m at $G{=}64$), whatever the modality (\cref{tab:modalities}).

\paragraph{Kernel floor.} At $G{=}64$, \cref{eq:ppt} with $k_{\min}{=}1$ gives LOLA roughness one pixel per token. Each token is then a scalar times a fixed vector, so all roughness tokens lie on a line in $\mathbb{R}^D$, a target that is easy to fit and carries little. A floor $k_{\min}{=}4$ pre-upsamples coarse modalities so that each token sees a $4\times4$ patch.

\paragraph{Convolutional stems.} A linear patch projection~\cite{dosovitskiy2021image} has to preserve edges and texture through one matrix, so our stems are factorized convolutions~\cite{xiao2021early}: $p_m$ is split into stride-3 and stride-2 stages ($9{=}3{\cdot}3$, $16{=}2^4$, $4{=}2^2$), each a convolution with GroupNorm and GELU, followed by a $1\times1$ projection to $D$. Resampling to $Gp_m$ pixels is antialiased. The usual $3\times3$ kernel with padding 1 centers output $o$ of a stride-$s$ stage on input pixel $so$ rather than on the center $so+(s{-}1)/2$ of its cell, a case of the receptive-field offsets that stride and padding produce~\cite{araujo2019computing,alsallakh2021mind}; the shifts add up across stages to
\begin{equation}
  \delta_m = -\frac{p_m-1}{2p_m}\ \text{cells},
  \label{eq:shift}
\end{equation}
that is $-0.38$, $-0.44$ and $-0.47$ of a token for $p_m = 4, 9, 16$, so tokens of different modalities would refer to ground up to 0.09 cells apart. A kernel of size $s{+}2$ with padding 1 centers each output on its cell, overlaps its neighbors at every stride and keeps the exact $G\times G$ output (\cref{sec:supp_stem}).

\subsection{Evidence encoder}
\label{sec:encoder}

The source enters through its stem, a per-token LayerNorm, a learned modality embedding $\mu_m$ and a location embedding $\lambda(\ell)$ that is added to every token,
\begin{equation}
  e_{m,i} = \LN\big(\phi_m(X_m)_i\big) + \mu_m + \lambda(\ell).
\end{equation}
$\lambda(\ell)$ represents the footprint center $\ell$ by Slepian functions~\cite{simons2006spatiospectral,rao2026localized}, a basis of $2D$ Slepian functions (1536 at $D{=}768$) built from spherical harmonics band-limited at $\ell_{\max}{=}140$, which resolves about $1.3^\circ$ or 40\,km, the scale of a footprint, and concentrated on a spherical cap of $18^\circ$ radius, passed through a two-layer MLP; the basis is evaluated once per footprint. The same vector is added to the target tokens through the EMA copy, so source and target share a bounded notion of where the footprint is. The source's own sun is withheld from the encoder.

If the encoder saw the whole source, the pretraining task would be too easy. Most of a target could then be answered cell by cell from the co-located source tokens, because a slope map says where the elevation map bends and a 643\,nm mosaic is close to a sunlit image, and the encoder would learn little beyond a per-cell lookup. We therefore hide part of the source, as I-JEPA does~\cite{assran2023self}, so that the encoder must also summarize what the visible ground implies about the hidden ground, and the predictor must fill the hidden cells from that summary rather than copy them. Hidden tokens are dropped from the sequence rather than replaced by mask tokens, as in MAE~\cite{he2022masked}, which saves compute and gives the encoder no placeholder to key on. The hidden region is the union of several random rectangles, placed anywhere in the grid so that every cell is hidden with the same probability rather than, as with a single central block, more often near the edges; their number, size and the resulting hidden fraction are set per run (\cref{sec:experiments}). The encoder $f_\theta$ is a pre-norm ViT-B~\cite{dosovitskiy2021image} (12 blocks, $D{=}768$, 12 heads) whose positions enter only through axial 2D rotary embeddings~\cite{su2024roformer,heo2024rotary} on the cell coordinates, half of each head rotated by the row and half by the column, so that tokens of different modalities at the same cell attend to each other as neighbors at offset zero. Queries and keys are LayerNormed per head before the rotation~\cite{dehghani2023scaling}. The last four blocks replace the MLP by a sparse mixture of 8 experts with top-2 routing; the router is a linear gate plus a bias per modality, with Gaussian noise on the logits during training and the importance and load balancing losses of V-MoE~\cite{riquelme2021scaling}. The encoder is read out at several depths rather than only at its last block, following the deep supervision of V-JEPA~2.1~\cite{murlabadia2026vjepa}. Early blocks keep local detail such as edges and texture, which a crater detector or a slope probe needs, while late blocks hold the more abstract summary that cross-modal prediction needs; a single final readout would force the predictor and every downstream head to work from the latter alone. Features from $L$ evenly spaced depths are normalized, concatenated and mixed by one linear layer into the evidence $z \in \mathbb{R}^{|V|\times D}$, where $V$ is the set of visible tokens.

\subsection{Target encoder}
\label{sec:target}

The predictor needs something to predict, and in a joint-embedding architecture that something is a representation rather than pixels. Representations must come from somewhere: if the student encoder produced its own targets, the loss could be driven to zero by mapping everything to a constant. Following BYOL and I-JEPA~\cite{grill2020bootstrap,assran2023self}, the targets come from a teacher that is an exponential moving average of the student, including its stems, embeddings and location MLP, so it is always a slightly older and smoother version of the same network and receives no gradient. The teacher reads each target observation in full, with nothing hidden, so that the target for a hidden cell is what the encoder would produce if it could see that cell. Being a copy of the student, it is read out at the same $L$ blocks and returns one target grid $\bar z^{\,l}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{G^2\times D}$ per depth $l$. The loss is then taken depth by depth: the prediction made for depth $l$ is compared with the teacher's depth-$l$ grid, so shallow features are matched to shallow ones and deep to deep. Each target is LayerNormed without affine parameters before the loss, which fixes its scale and keeps the student from shrinking its predictions instead of improving them.

\subsection{A predictor that is told the sun}
\label{sec:predictor}

The predictor renders: given the evidence, it predicts what modality $t$ looks like at every cell of the footprint under the sun $g_t$. It is a transformer decoder with one query per cell. Each query is a learned vector plus the target modality embedding $\mu_t$, placed at its cell by the rotary embedding; it reads the evidence by cross-attention and the other queries by self-attention, so a hidden cell is filled from its visible neighbors and from the footprint as a whole. The evidence is read-only. Unlike I-JEPA's narrow predictor~\cite{assran2023self}, which only fills holes in one image, ours has the encoder's width, because translating between modalities is closer to rendering.

The sun has to reach every layer. The six angles of $g_t$ are encoded as sines and cosines, times a validity bit that is zero for static targets, so ``no sun'' is a distinct input rather than a sun at zenith. An MLP maps them to a condition vector $c_t$ that scales and shifts every LayerNorm of the predictor (adaptive LayerNorm~\cite{peebles2023scalable}, initialized to the identity) and is appended to the evidence as one extra key--value token. One head per supervision depth maps the final query states to $\hat z^{\,l}_t$.

\subsection{Objective}
\label{sec:objective}

Predictions are compared with targets by the smooth-$\ell_1$ (Huber) loss, $\rho(u) = u^2/2$ for $|u| < 1$ and $|u| - 1/2$ otherwise, which is quadratic near zero and linear far from it, so that a few badly predicted features do not dominate the gradient. With $\LN$ a LayerNorm without affine parameters, the observation loss for target $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{obs}} = \frac{1}{L}\sum_{l=1}^{L} \frac{1}{G^2 D}\sum_{i,d}
  \rho\big(\hat z^{\,l}_{t,i,d} - \LN(\sg[\bar z^{\,l}_{t,i}])_d\big),
  \label{eq:cm}
\end{equation}
with $\sg$ the stop-gradient. Following V-JEPA~2.1~\cite{murlabadia2026vjepa} we add a dense context loss that pulls each visible source token, at every depth, toward the EMA encoding of the full, unmasked source at the same cell,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{ctx}} = \frac{1}{L}\sum_{l} \frac{1}{|V|}\sum_{i\in V} w_i\, \frac{1}{D}\sum_d \rho\big(z^{l}_{i,d} - \LN(\bar z^{\,l}_{s,i})_d\big),
  \label{eq:ctx}
\end{equation}
where $z^{l}_i$ is the encoder's depth-$l$ feature of token $i$ before fusion and $w_i \propto e^{-(d_i-1)/\tau}$, with $d_i$ the distance in cells to the nearest hidden cell and $\tau{=}2$, normalized to mean one. Tokens next to the hidden region, where the full view knows more than the masked one, carry most of the weight, and the loss keeps visible tokens local instead of letting them turn into global aggregators. VICReg variance and covariance terms~\cite{bardes2022vicreg}, $\mathcal{V}$ and $\mathcal{C}$, act on the evidence $z$ and on the final-depth predictions $\hat z^{\,L}$. An auxiliary head regresses from $\lambda(\ell)$ the footprint means of Diviner bolometric temperature and GRAIL gravity, the two layers too coarse to tokenize, with an $\ell_1$ loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cond}}$. The full objective is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{L} = {} & \sum_t \mathcal{L}_{\text{obs}} + \lambda_{\text{ctx}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{ctx}}
  + w_v\,\big[\mathcal{V}(z) + \mathcal{V}(\hat z^{\,L})\big] \\
  & + w_c\,\big[\mathcal{C}(z) + \mathcal{C}(\hat z^{\,L})\big] + \alpha\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{moe}} + \beta\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{cond}},
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:total}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_{\text{ctx}}{=}0.5$, $w_v{=}1$, $w_c{=}0.005$, $\alpha{=}10^{-3}$ on the summed balancing terms and $\beta{=}0.01$. The EMA decay is 0.996.

\subsection{Tasks}
\label{sec:tasks}

A training row is one footprint with one of its WAC observations and the static maps of the footprint. The modalities present in the row are shuffled uniformly; the first becomes the source and the next two the targets, so every modality serves as source and as target equally often and targets never appear among the sources. A camera target is predicted under the sun of the row's observation, which the predictor is told and the encoder is not; a camera source shares that sun. Sub-tile rows add NAC and the 3\,m stereo terrain under the same rule. A task mixture by family, in which imagery predicts terrain and composition, normalized reflectance predicts imagery under a sun, and terrain predicts its own derivatives, can replace the uniform draw with set probabilities; a minimum sampling frequency per modality then keeps rare modalities in the mixture.
